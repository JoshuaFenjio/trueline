## Two things the brief assumed that weren't true

**Role×country and role×city pages did not exist.** Role×country was a
client-side panel inside the role map; role×city was the homepage query
`/?role=X&city=Y` (listed in the old sitemap). With sign-off, they are now
real server-rendered routes: `/roles/[role]/country/[place]` and
`/roles/[role]/city/[place]` — 163 + 41 publishing today.

**Google no longer renders several of the targeted rich results.**
Estimated Salary (Occupation) was retired in June 2025 — its spec page now
redirects to the Search Central changelog; FAQ rich results stopped in 2026;
the Sitelinks search box (WebSite/SearchAction) in 2024. With sign-off, all
of it is emitted anyway — it is valid schema.org, other engines and AI
crawlers read it, and it doesn't hurt rankings — but **nobody should expect a
Google rich result from Occupation, FAQPage or SearchAction.** BreadcrumbList
and Dataset are still live Google features.

## Decisions and deviations from the brief

- **`unitText: YEAR` → `duration: "P1Y"`.** MonetaryAmountDistribution has
  no `unitText`; the (retired) Google spec and schema.org express "per year"
  as `duration`. `sampleSize` is emitted as asked; it was a Google-extension
  property, so the schema.org validator may warn on it.
- **No OccupationAggregationByEmployer.** None of the item-1 pages
  aggregates by a single employer (they aggregate across employers). Company
  pages would, but their gate is n≥3 — too thin for a percentile
  distribution — so they carry none.
- **No Occupation for "Other" or group families** (Operations, Finance,
  Marketing…). Those are functions, not occupations; marking them up as one
  Occupation would misstate what the number measures. They still get the
  answer-first sentence and FAQ (except "Other", which gets neither). One
  predicate (`occupationEligible` in `lib/seoCopy.ts`) controls this.
- **EMEA-wide pages' occupationLocation** is a `Place` named "Europe, the
  Middle East and Africa (EMEA)" — honest, but not the City/State/Country
  the old Google spec required.
- **No `sameAs` on Organization.** The footer's X/LinkedIn/GitHub icons all
  point to `#`. Add real profile URLs in `organizationLd()` when they exist.
- **Answer-first "N"** is the salaried sample behind the median, not total
  ads, so the sentence is literally true: "…from 54 salaried live job ads…".
- **Every schema value is rounded once to €1,000 and shared with the visible
  figure** (new percentile strip under the stat cards), so markup == page.

## Disclosure rates: a correction you should look at

Five sources are salary-filtered at ingest (`new_sources.py`: Himalayas,
Landing.jobs, GermanTechJobs, ITJobs, plus Adzuna/Reed scaffolds) — 12,343 of
27,293 live ads. Counting them in a *rate* made the stats page read **59%**
of job ads disclose pay (Germany **85%**). On employers' own job boards it is
**25%** (Germany **19%**). The stats page and the new FAQ answers use the
employer-board basis and say so.

**Not changed, needs your call:** the existing "Job ads that disclose pay"
stat cards on city/country/company pages and the leaderboard "transparency"
column still use all feeds. They are labelled "of all live ads here", which
is literally true, but they read as market rates. Switching them to
`fullFeed` rows is a one-line change per site in `lib/data.ts`.

## Audit flags (17 Nov review)

- **a.** "Other" removed from the homepage spotlight rotation pool.
- **b.** Already in place since `ec05f21` (flagship gate n≥15). Verified on
  production: the insight features **Switzerland (n=24)** while **Romania
  (n=9)** tops the n≥8 table below it. Fixed stale copy that said the leader
  appears "at the 8-salaried-ad gate".
- **c. EWOR GmbH — no rule excludes it; not special-cased.** 5 active ads,
  all via Himalayas, all "Co-Founder" placements (an accelerator). The 3 that
  count are an identical **€500,000–€500,000** annual band → €500k midpoint at
  n=3 → Pay Index 100. Why every existing rule lets it through:
  - the only boilerplate *exclusion* is the monthly-band rule (annualises
    under €35k) — these are annual;
  - the "identical band repeated" signature is a report-only diagnostic and
    fires at ≥5 repeats — EWOR has 3;
  - the plausibility ceiling rejects `> €500,000`; exactly €500,000 passes.

  The other 2 ads are already excluded (`parsed_suspect`, width gate). A
  general fix you might choose: make the ceiling exclusive (`>= 500,000`), or
  promote the identical-band signature to an exclusion at n≥3 within one
  company. Either would apply to every company, not just EWOR.

Related thin headline worth knowing: **Romania ranks #1 by median (€115,000)
on 9 salaried ads, 4 of them MetaWealth.** It clears the existing n≥8 gate so
it still ranks; the new countries-hub meta description no longer names a
leader, and the stats page headlines only countries with 15+ salaried ads.
Berlin Software Engineer (€107,000) also sits exactly at n=8.
